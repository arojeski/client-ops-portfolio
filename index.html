The short version

Basically?
I'm a nerd translator.

Technology keeps getting more powerful. That doesn't automatically make it easier for people to understand, adopt, or use well.

That's the gap I naturally gravitate toward. I can get into the weeds with a technical problem, then come back out and explain what matters in normal human language. Just as importantly, I can listen to a client or teammate, figure out what they're actually trying to accomplish, and translate that need back into something actionable.

As organizations adopt more automation and AI, I think that bridge matters more than ever. The best technology isn't just technically impressive—it has to make sense for the humans on the other side of it.

Understand the technology.→Understand the person.→Connect the two.

My approach

Technology should fit people.
Not the other way around.

01

Ask before assuming.
The stated problem and the actual need aren't always the same thing.

02

Lose the jargon.
If the explanation only works for people who already understand it, it's not a very useful explanation.

03

Make ownership obvious.
Everyone should know what's happening, who owns what, and what comes next.

04

Stay curious.
I don't need to be the deepest technical expert in the room. I need to understand enough to ask the right questions and connect the right people.
